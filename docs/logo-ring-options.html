Reference only. This is SOP62 candidate A with the exact final geometry from logo-options.html (both cuts and the motion are unchanged). It is not the approved logo.

Close · ring and separate note, different structures

A2 · Pen ring

Opening idea: Imani. Revised in cycle 1 (Mara, Imani).

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16, no accent

Static. A’s gap and separate note, but the ring changes weight. It runs from about 4.75 at lower left to about 2.75 near the gap. At 64px and above that is a gentle modulation. The 16px cut deliberately has more contrast (about 5.5 to 3) so it doesn’t fall back into A at small sizes.

Tests. Does uneven line weight remove the spinner/refresh reading that an even arc has?

Cycle 1. The swell was cut from 5.75 to 4.75, because at 32px and up the crescent plus the dot read as a crescent and star (Mara). The 16px contrast was raised, because at 16 the first version looked like A (Imani).

Motion. None. This is a static study only.

Risk. It may still read as a crescent. At 24px it is only slightly distinct from A.

A3 · Closed ring, outside note

Opening idea: Marcus. Kept for one cycle as a test (Marcus’s call; Imani would cut it).

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16, no accent

Static. A’s exact ring, closed, with the same r2.75 note on the same 45° line but outside the ring, 2 units clear. Closing the gap removes the arc that reads as refresh.

Tests. Does A’s gap do any work? This candidate is A with only the gap closed: same ring, same 45° direction, and the note moved just outside.

Cycle 1. The ring is now A’s ring exactly (it was a smaller, offset ring). That makes A versus A3 a clean, single-variable comparison.

Motion. None. This is a static study only.

Risk. In the cycle-1 renders, all three voices read it as something else: a notification badge, the ♂ symbol, or an extra “o·” after the wordmark. It is still here to put that cost next to A’s refresh risk.

Adjacent · different premise, same note grammar

N2 · Bite

Opening idea: Imani. Revised in cycle 1.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16, no accent

Static. A filled disc the size of A’s outer edge (r12.25). A’s note (r2.75) sits in exactly A’s position, in a round notch with an even 2.25 clearance. It shares A’s grammar, filled in.

Tests. Is a solid form stronger and more ownable than a line (reversed, on app tiles, without colour), or does the avatar-status-badge reading win?

N3 · Column and note

Opening idea: Marcus. Revised in cycle 1.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16, no accent

Static. A rounded upright bar with a round notch in its right edge. The note sits in the notch with the same 2.25 clearance as N2. We read the bar as a column of text and the note as a margin comment, but that reading is untested.

Tests. Does the circle do any work? This keeps the note-in-a-notch grammar and drops only the ring.

Cycle 1. In cycle 1 the note floated beside the bar and read as an “i” at 16px (Mara). It is now set into a notch, so the two shapes lock together the same way as in N2.

I · Brief territory 1 — Glossed Page · opening pass: Mara · critiqued and revised by all three voices

Gloss

The brand is the architecture of commentary around a text. Your page is the central column; Sophia speaks in the margin, tied to the line she comments on by a small rubric reference point.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only in the Almanac masthead. The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page.

Why the marks belong
Gloss bracket (Sophia): a heavy column (the text), a square reference point on its top line, and a thin bracket for commentary. Surround (Almanac): the text block closed on three sides by commentary — a finished, framed entry, open only at the foot. Both draw a reading relation, not an object.
Provenance roles
Source = indented block with SOURCE numeral; Yours = heavy left rule + YOURS; Sophia = smaller roman in the gloss column with rubric small-caps label. Rubric appears on labels, reference points and the selection; error uses ochre + ⚠ + words. Dark is not an inversion: hairlines vanish at night, so the margin becomes a raised plane on cool graphite. Below 620px the gloss stays beside its line as an indented gloss with a hanging rubric letter.
Type
Hoefler Text (fallback Baskerville, Georgia) roman/italic/small caps; UI labels in the same family.
Excluded
No manuscript texture, blackletter, daggers/pilcrows as ornament, or religious decoration.
Risk & test
At 16px the bracket may read as code. Rubric red may read as a correction pen. Test greyscale 16px, and whether the 390px interleaved gloss (hanging rubric letters) still reads as commentary.

Slip & Interpolation

Weeks later you must see at a glance whose sentence is whose. Sources are numbered slips; the page and your words stay plain ink; only Sophia’s interpretations are bracketed — the editorial convention for someone speaking inside another’s text.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only in the Almanac masthead. The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page.

Why the marks belong
Interpolation [ . ] (Sophia): square brackets around a full stop — where Sophia adds to, but does not overwrite, your text. Dated slip (Almanac): the slip’s heavy heading rule over a single full stop — one finished idea per slip (risk: reads as a calendar page).
Provenance roles
Accent marks only Sophia’s own voice — her interpretations and the Almanac entries she prepares — and the current selection (an outline, no fill, no badge). Each role also carries a word label and bracket glyph; sources carry numerals that link to their slip. Error = red + ⚠ + “not opened”.
Type
Avenir Next (labels, wordmark, tabular numerals) with Charter for reading.
Excluded
No tag chips, category colours, status pills, graphs, streak counts or PKM link-graph imagery.
Risk & test
Violet replaced Marcus’s original red to keep this distinct from Gloss; he accepted the structure in violet but flags saturated violet as a generic ‘AI’ colour — compare the muted variant. Brackets may read as code at 16px.

Night Scale

A scale with one reading taken. Drawn on dark first; light is derived. Sophia’s Almanac shares the geometry but closes it into a frame with a large year and no pointer, because its entries are finished.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only in the Almanac masthead. The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page. The premise above (“A scale with one reading taken…”) and the “rings enclosing the mark” exclusion are also round-two statements about the old scale mark. They are kept as reference and are not re-decided here, even though ring candidates A, B and C conflict with that exclusion.

Why the marks belong
Scale & caret (Sophia): a baseline with seven uneven ticks; the third is tall with a lamp-coloured caret — one reading taken. 16px and 24px optical cuts keep the caret and three ticks so it never collapses to a bare ⊥. Frame (Almanac): the ticks closed into a frame under a heavy year band. The inspected 1851 almanac cover supports only a closed frame, a central title and a prominent year; the ticks are this territory’s own geometry, and issues are never numbered.
Provenance roles
A gutter scale: each block has a tick and a word (Page, You, Source n, Sophia). The tall tick marks the passage under discussion. Lamp marks Sophia, the selection and the reading caret; error = coral + ⚠ + words. Below 620px the gutter word moves above its block.
Type
Palatino upright for wordmark and reading; Menlo/PT Mono for datelines, gutter words and labels.
Excluded
No glow, gradients, moons, stars, rings enclosing the mark, mountains or astrology.
Risk & test
The scale may read as a slider, progress bar or volume control. Test: unlabeled 16px mark, five people; any “progress”/“volume” answer fails.

IV · Brief territory 6 — Patina (restraint successor to Margin) · opening pass: brief text · critiqued and revised by all three voices

Colophon

The brand recedes so your titles lead. A quiet lowercase wordmark, a green thread beside the passage you have selected to discuss, and attributions written as plain trailing words, the way a colophon credits a book.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only at the end of the Almanac entry (this masthead has no mark). The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page.

Why the marks belong
End mark (Sophia): a rule that comes to rest on a square — the typographic sign that a piece is complete for now. Last line (Almanac): lines of a finished entry, the last one ending on the square. The earlier ribbon was cut: it read as a ‘T’ and implied a bookmark feature that doesn’t exist.
Provenance roles
Roles are carried by posture and a trailing attribution (“— you”, “— source, opened”, “— Sophia, uncertain”), so they survive with colour off. Thread green appears only on the selection, the mark and Sophia’s name label; it never carries a role on its own. Open question (Marcus): trailing labels may be read too late.
Type
Gill Sans (wordmark, labels) with PT Serif for reading.
Excluded
No leather, distress, ‘wear’, usage counts, mastery or progress implied by the thread.
Risk & test
The quiet control: tests whether “none of the logos were good” meant “less logo”. May be too quiet in a Dock. Test: icon among ten Dock neighbours at 32px; can people find it twice?
