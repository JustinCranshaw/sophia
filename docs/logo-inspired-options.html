Related parts · added in the scope correction, not ranked

RL · Relay

Proposed by Mara in the scope correction (related parts).

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. Five parts on a ring band (radii 7–13). Each part ends in a rounded head (r3) that nests in a scoop (r4.4) cut into the back of the next part, leaving an even 1.4 curved gap at every joint. The 16px cut uses a wider band (6.5–13.5), a 3.5 head and a 5.9 scoop, so each joint gap is 2.4.

Nearest favourite. Gather: its parts are separate capsules with plain gaps. Relay’s parts lock into one another through curved joints, so the ring reads as a chain with a direction rather than a set of pieces.

This cycle. New in this cycle. Built to Mara’s numbers, except that the scoop tips are left pointed rather than rounded to 0.8.

Tests. Can the joint between parts, rather than the number of parts, carry a related-parts identity at 16px?

Risk. Mara’s own guesses: a loading spinner, cashews, or hands held in a circle.

HD · Huddle

Proposed by Imani in the scope correction (related parts).

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. A disc (r14) with a round opening (r6) shifted 4.95 toward 1:30. Straight 1.8-wide gaps radiate from the opening’s centre and divide it into five parts. Because the opening is off-centre, no two parts are the same size, and the lower left is heavier. Corners are rounded at 1.2. Imani proposed 1.8, but the thinnest wall (3.05) cannot take more. The 16px cut uses a 5.5 opening shifted 3.5, 2.6 gaps and 1.4 corners.

Nearest favourite. Roundtable has five soft parts around an opening. Huddle drops the repetition: the off-centre opening makes every part different and gives the mark a direction.

This cycle. New in this cycle. Built to Imani’s numbers apart from the corner radius noted above.

Tests. Does the user’s liking for related parts come from equal rhythm, or from parts gathered around a space? Huddle keeps the gathering and breaks the rhythm.

Risk. Imani’s guess: a misaligned Roundtable or an iris. In our renders it can also look like a cut pie or a beach ball at 64px and above, and it is busy at 16px.

Solid contrasts · from the opening round, not ranked

GR · Groove

Opening idea: Imani. Revised in cycle 1 (Imani, Mara). Solid contrast.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. A solid disc (r12.75) with a single 2.2-wide channel. It enters at the rim at about 7 o’clock (lower left), turns clockwise about 265° as it moves inward, and ends in a round opening (r2.7) to the right of centre. The 16px cut uses a 3.2 channel and a 3.6 opening.

Nearest favourite. The cut into a solid disc is like Cove. The inward turn is like Ripple, turned inside out: an empty channel in solid mass, not a line.

Cycle 1. Rotated so the channel leaves the rim at lower left. Imani read v1’s upper-right exit as drifting toward Cove’s bite at 16px. Mara widened the 16px channel and opening.

Tests. The inward turn as empty space inside solid mass. A reading as an ear, a snail, a “6” or a “G” before it reads as a mark would count against it.

Risk. Our hypothesis is that it may read as a “G” or an ear. The wall above the channel is thin near the top. Parked Curl is its figure–ground pair.

ST · Strata

Opening idea: Imani. Revised in cycle 1 (Imani, Mara). Solid contrast.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. A solid disc (r12.75) cut by three gently sagging seams, 1.9 wide. They enter from the left rim and stop at three different lengths, ragged right, so none crosses the disc. The 16px cut uses 3.0-wide seams and keeps the last seam short.

Nearest favourite. Passage is also a disc cut by a seam. Strata has three short seams from one side instead of one S-shaped seam across, and no centre.

Cycle 1. In v1 two seams crossed the whole disc. Imani read that as the striped-globe signature of an existing telecom mark and called it disqualifying as drawn. She proposed Strata, so she asked for her view to be discounted. The seams now stop short. Mara widened them at 16px.

Tests. Is a mark with no centre, made of lines rather than radial parts, worth having when every favourite is radial? A striped-globe reading, or a menu-icon reading next to the wordmark, would count against it.

Risk. At 16px it may still read as a menu (hamburger) icon or a striped ball (Marcus, Mara). The ragged ends are hard to see below 24px. Whether the globe resemblance is gone is our reading, not a check.

LN · Lens

Opening idea: Marcus. Revised in cycle 1 (Imani). Solid contrast.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Full-bleed tile variant (cycle 1) · 16 · 32 · 48

Static. A rounded square (24 wide, corner radius 8) with one almond-shaped opening, 13.2 long and 5.8 wide, tilted at 45° and set up and to the right of centre. It is the only candidate that is not round. The 16px cut uses a slightly larger square and a wider opening (7.2).

Nearest favourite. Offset and Cove each have one opening in a solid shape. This changes the outline (rounded square, not disc), the opening (almond, not circle), and where it sits (fully inside, not breaking the edge).

Cycle 1. On the app tiles, Imani read v1 as a tile inside a tile, which hides the outline idea. This version adds a full-bleed variant (above): the outline is the tile itself, and the almond scales with it. The context sheets still show the standard mark on the shared tile.

Tests. Can a non-round outline carry the identity when every favourite is round? A leaf reading, or the tile-in-tile look, would count against it.

Risk. The opening may read as a leaf (Mara called this her own taste). Leaving the circle drops the one thing all the favourites share.

Parked · kept for comparison, with the reason and any dissent

SI · Step In

Proposed by Marcus in the scope correction (related parts). Parked in this cycle.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. Four soft seats (radii 8.5–14, 2.4 parallel gaps, corners 1.6) in a ring of five positions. The fifth seat, at the top, is copied at half scale into the middle, just under the empty slot, so the slot and the inner part share a shape. The 16px cut uses radii 9–15, 3.2 gaps and an inner seat at radii 3–7.

Nearest favourite. Roundtable has seats around a centre. Open table (board 4, not a favourite) also leaves a slot open. Here the missing seat moves inward instead.

Why parked. Parked by the lead before the final read. At 16 and 24px the inner seat shrinks to a small separate part under a gap in an open ring. In our reading that comes close to the old A · Margin ring (an open ring with a small part at the gap), which the brief says not to force. Marcus’s own guess was a paw print or a face. It can still be applied for comparison. In the final read Marcus, who proposed it, agreed: to him it reads as a face.

VN · Vane

Opening idea: Mara. Parked in cycle 1.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. A solid disc (r12.75) cut by six curved seams, each an arc of the disc’s own radius from the centre to the rim, 1.9 wide, with a small centre opening (r2.4). Unchanged from v1.

Nearest favourite. Sixfold and Soft six share the six-way division. Here the division is cuts through one solid, not separate tiles around a void.

Why parked. Parked by Mara, who proposed it. Small-size craft is her call. At 16px the six 1.25px seams and the centre opening break into speckle, the same failure that cut Perforate. In her reading, the shutter look comes from all the cuts meeting at the centre. Imani judged the structure meaningfully new relative to the favourites. Mara would reopen it only as a version with no centre opening and a solid hub. It can still be applied to the contexts for comparison.

CU · Curl

Opening idea: Marcus. Parked in cycle 1.

16 · 24 · 32 · mono 32 · mono 16

Static. A ring (r10.6, 3.6 line) in two unequal pieces. The long piece turns inward on a tighter radius and ends in a solid lobe (r3.3) near the centre, and a short arc sits at the right. Unchanged from v1.

Nearest favourite. Ripple has open arcs turning inward. Imani read Curl as the closest of the five to a trace: Ripple with one ring removed.

Why parked. Parked on portfolio grounds by Imani, whose call it is, and by Marcus, who proposed it. All three voices read Curl and Groove as one gesture with figure and ground swapped. Dissent: Mara would keep both as a deliberate test of line against mass. She proposed a 16px fix (the short arc moved clear of the hook, and a smaller hook radius), which was not built. It can still be applied to the contexts for comparison.

I · Brief territory 1 — Glossed Page · opening pass: Mara · critiqued and revised by all three voices

Gloss

The brand is the architecture of commentary around a text. Your page is the central column; Sophia speaks in the margin, tied to the line she comments on by a small rubric reference point.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only in the Almanac masthead. The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page. All candidates here are ink-only, so fact lines that give the mark an accent colour or a caret also describe the round-two marks.

Why the marks belong
Gloss bracket (Sophia): a heavy column (the text), a square reference point on its top line, and a thin bracket for commentary. Surround (Almanac): the text block closed on three sides by commentary — a finished, framed entry, open only at the foot. Both draw a reading relation, not an object.
Provenance roles
Source = indented block with SOURCE numeral; Yours = heavy left rule + YOURS; Sophia = smaller roman in the gloss column with rubric small-caps label. Rubric appears on labels, reference points and the selection; error uses ochre + ⚠ + words. Dark is not an inversion: hairlines vanish at night, so the margin becomes a raised plane on cool graphite. Below 620px the gloss stays beside its line as an indented gloss with a hanging rubric letter.
Type
Hoefler Text (fallback Baskerville, Georgia) roman/italic/small caps; UI labels in the same family.
Excluded
No manuscript texture, blackletter, daggers/pilcrows as ornament, or religious decoration.
Risk & test
At 16px the bracket may read as code. Rubric red may read as a correction pen. Test greyscale 16px, and whether the 390px interleaved gloss (hanging rubric letters) still reads as commentary.

Excluded

Night Scale

A scale with one reading taken. Drawn on dark first; light is derived. Sophia’s Almanac shares the geometry but closes it into a frame with a large year and no pointer, because its entries are finished.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only in the Almanac masthead. The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page. All candidates here are ink-only, so fact lines that give the mark an accent colour or a caret also describe the round-two marks. The premise above (“A scale with one reading taken…”) and the “rings enclosing the mark” exclusion are also round-two statements about the old scale mark. They are kept as reference and are not re-decided here, even though the ring-shaped candidates here (RL Relay, HD Huddle, SI Step In, CU Curl) conflict with that exclusion.

Why the marks belong
Scale & caret (Sophia): a baseline with seven uneven ticks; the third is tall with a lamp-coloured caret — one reading taken. 16px and 24px optical cuts keep the caret and three ticks so it never collapses to a bare ⊥. Frame (Almanac): the ticks closed into a frame under a heavy year band. The inspected 1851 almanac cover supports only a closed frame, a central title and a prominent year; the ticks are this territory’s own geometry, and issues are never numbered.
Provenance roles
A gutter scale: each block has a tick and a word (Page, You, Source n, Sophia). The tall tick marks the passage under discussion. Lamp marks Sophia, the selection and the reading caret; error = coral + ⚠ + words. Below 620px the gutter word moves above its block.
Type
Palatino upright for wordmark and reading; Menlo/PT Mono for datelines, gutter words and labels.
Excluded
No glow, gradients, moons, stars, rings enclosing the mark, mountains or astrology.
Risk & test
The scale may read as a slider, progress bar or volume control. Test: unlabeled 16px mark, five people; any “progress”/“volume” answer fails.

IV · Brief territory 6 — Patina (restraint successor to Margin) · opening pass: brief text · critiqued and revised by all three voices

Colophon

The brand recedes so your titles lead. A quiet lowercase wordmark, a green thread beside the passage you have selected to discuss, and attributions written as plain trailing words, the way a colophon credits a book.

Fixed context, copied unchanged from round two. Only the Sophia mark is swapped for the selected candidate, which also appears ink-only at the end of the Almanac entry (this masthead has no mark). The facts below are verbatim, so “Why the marks belong” and parts of “Risk & test” still describe the round-two marks, which are not shown on this page. All candidates here are ink-only, so fact lines that give the mark an accent colour or a caret also describe the round-two marks.

Why the marks belong
End mark (Sophia): a rule that comes to rest on a square — the typographic sign that a piece is complete for now. Last line (Almanac): lines of a finished entry, the last one ending on the square. The earlier ribbon was cut: it read as a ‘T’ and implied a bookmark feature that doesn’t exist.
Provenance roles
Roles are carried by posture and a trailing attribution (“— you”, “— source, opened”, “— Sophia, uncertain”), so they survive with colour off. Thread green appears only on the selection, the mark and Sophia’s name label; it never carries a role on its own. Open question (Marcus): trailing labels may be read too late.
Type
Gill Sans (wordmark, labels) with PT Serif for reading.
Excluded
No leather, distress, ‘wear’, usage counts, mastery or progress implied by the thread.
Risk & test
The quiet control: tests whether “none of the logos were good” meant “less logo”. May be too quiet in a Dock. Test: icon among ten Dock neighbours at 32px; can people find it twice?

How these were made

Principle. The three voices read the four boards independently and reached much the same idea: one solid, mostly round shape whose identity comes from the empty space cut into it, kept wide enough to survive at 16px in one colour. This is their reading, not a finding. The opening voices also excluded rings of related parts because the favourites already cover them. That exclusion was reversed in the scope correction below.

Opening. Mara, Marcus and Imani each read their persona file, the brief and all four boards, then proposed two families. All six were drawn and rendered. One was cut on that first render, before the page was built: PF Perforate (Mara), whose holes merged into speckle at 16px. At larger sizes it read as a button, and its rim-breaking hole was close to Offset.

Cycle 1. The opening voices could not be resumed. Three disclosed replacement voices each read their persona file, the brief, all four boards and the v1 renders, then critiqued, each starting from its own earlier position plus digests of the other two. The renders were a per-candidate board cloned into all eight faces, a 16–128px sheet, and real context sections. Vane and Curl were parked (see their cards). Lens, Strata and Groove were each revised once to sharpen how they differ. No voice proposed a credible sixth mark, so none was added. Imani raised one open question without proposing a mark: four of the five marks are discs, so at 16px they differ only inside the disc. Should the outline carry the identity instead?

Scope correction. Checking v2 against the brief showed a gap. The opening voices had excluded the soft segmented, related-parts family, yet four of the eight favourites belong to it: Gather, Roundtable, Sixfold and Soft six. “Inspiration only” rules out tracing, not the family. Three disclosed replacement voices each re-read their persona file and all four boards. They agreed the exclusion had confused originality with avoiding a family the user likes, and each proposed one new related-parts construction. All three were built and rendered: Relay (interlocking joints), Huddle (unequal parts around an off-centre opening) and Step In (one seat moved into the middle). Relay and Huddle are main options. Step In is parked (see its card).

Final read (the voices’ hypotheses, not tests). All three voices inspected the finished page. Mara recorded a dissent: at 16–24px neither Relay nor Huddle is clearly distinct yet, Relay’s joints may merge into a familiar notched ring close to Gather or Sixfold, and Huddle may read as a beach ball. Marcus noted a gap: both new marks are five-part rings near Roundtable, and nothing echoes Sixfold or Soft six. He accepted Step In’s parking. Imani judged Relay and Huddle new relative to the favourites at larger rendered sizes, as separate bets on connected parts and on direction. No human recognition test at real size or in greyscale has been run.

Tests proposed by the voices (not run)

  • Mara (design). The favourites may share a six-part count rather than cut-out space. Test with an unlabelled 16px mono strip of these marks next to a six-tile ring.
  • Marcus (product). The idea that the 16px favicon matters most is untested. Put the marks at 16px in a row of browser tabs and ask the user to rank them. Check whether Strata next to the wordmark reads as a menu button.
  • Imani (CEO). The user may love the rhythm of repeated tiles, not the cuts. At 24px in Colophon, ask for a five-second pick between these marks and a tile ring.
