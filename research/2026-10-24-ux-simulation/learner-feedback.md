# Learner retrospectives

The original Sophia app windows had been closed. At the user's request, the four original learner agents were re-contacted in their existing contexts and asked what was easy to learn, what was hard, and how the experience could improve. They were also asked what remained unlearned, which teaching interaction mattered most, how the book and notes affected learning, and whether they would return for a concrete task.

These are reflective simulated interviews grounded in the earlier sessions. They are not new Sophia interactions, human interviews, independent assessments of mastery, or delayed-retention tests. No app was relaunched, no new provider setup was attempted, and no additional learner submissions were added to the original counts. Quotes below are the agents' retrospective statements, not statements from human participants.

## Elena — eigenvectors and vibration modes

**What became easier.** Elena could separate a fixed pattern from a fixed length. Shrinking and reversing motion no longer contradicted the idea of a mode. She could explain why `(2, −2)` has the same mode shape as `(1, −1)` with twice the amplitude.

> A mode keeps a pattern, not a length.

The most helpful interaction was the explanation of a kick to only one mass: unequal velocities immediately change the displacement ratio. That supplied a physical reason she could picture. The `(1, 0)` counterexample also helped because the second mass has zero displacement but nonzero acceleration, so that pattern cannot persist.

**What remained hard.** Elena had not worked through the eigenvalue-condition derivation herself or explained why the eigenvalue corresponds to frequency squared. The building-motion extension and damping/nonlinearity caveats were things she had heard, not demonstrated understanding of. She distinguished restating and extending an explanation on screen from measured mastery.

The first response did too much at once: a long page, criteria and records arrived around a request for a tiny example. Later, a missing explanation forced her to repeat the kick question. Repairing the note that misattributed Sophia's phase wording to her own misunderstanding also consumed attention.

> I wanted to think about the masses, not manage the account of what I understood.

**What would improve the next visit.** Answer the current question before notebook housekeeping. Give one small explanation, leave room for her response, then consider what deserves preserving. The book became valuable once her own engineering reminder anchored the longer explanation and survived revision. The conversation was useful for predictions and uncertainty; reviewing the notes sometimes competed with that work.

**Would she return?** Yes: derive the eigenvalue condition from the same two-mass example, one step at a time, with Elena trying each step and explaining its physical meaning before deciding whether the page needs changing.

## Jamal — Reconstruction and citizenship

**What became easier.** Jamal arrived treating emancipation, citizenship and rights as one bundle, with Reconstruction vaguely associated with rebuilding. He left with four separate questions: legal freedom, citizenship, voting and practical enforcement.

> I arrived with one bucket called freedom. I left with four questions.

The concrete example of a person trying to register to vote made the gap between constitutional text and institutional protection understandable. He wrote his own reminder into the page because the distinction could help a book discussion. Sophia's willingness to accept his challenge to “legally nobody” and revise the explanation made questioning her productive.

**What remained hard.** Jamal could not yet lead a confident comparison of historians' arguments about Reconstruction's failure. He understood the proposed distinction between immediate political failure and a surviving constitutional legacy, but had not read the promised passages. He also needed to apply the framework to particular places and people without generalizing their experiences.

The broad replies could substitute a wall of explanation for the wall of dates he had declined. A three-sentence reply was easier to use. The book and chat could split attention before the basic idea settled; record updates distracted, while preserved corrections reassured him. Source candor was valuable but did not deliver inspectable evidence.

> I understood the explanation before I could trust the evidence behind it.

**What would improve the next visit.** Build the session around two short passages that both the learner and Sophia can actually read, with the author's words visibly separate from Sophia's interpretation. Explanation clarity and source reliability need separate treatment.

**Would he return?** Yes: compare what two historians mean by “failure,” then formulate a thoughtful discussion question for his group.

## Theo — rhythm from staff and tab

**What became easier.** Theo first read a half note as two plucks and wondered whether the tab's empty space meant a rest. After seeing held-note and rest versions side by side, he could describe the difference: ONE pluck G, two let it ring, THREE pluck E, FOUR pluck C; in the rest version, stop G on two.

> I finally understood that I still count the beat when my picking hand does nothing.

The contrasting pictures were more useful than another paragraph about note values. A later concise exchange resolved his beats-versus-seconds confusion: the half note lasts two clicks, so two beats at 80 take one and a half seconds. Sophia respected his request not to make another page for that quick question.

**What remained hard.** Translating the explanation into hands-on timing remained completely untested. Theo did not know whether he could keep counting while changing strings, resist another pluck on two, or retain the duration at a different tempo. He valued the notes and briefing retaining that distinction rather than treating correct on-screen reasoning as playing ability.

The practice card helped because it put the phrase, count and his picking-hand reminder together. Editing exposed notation code and formatting for a simple sentence, which he described as intimidating. Notes and repeated closing replies interrupted the conversation's rhythm. Having to correct “reads tab fluently” also diverted attention, though preserving the correction helped recover trust.

**What would improve the next visit.** Take him directly from the return message to the relevant bar and practice instruction, rather than making him locate the card after a correct recollection.

> Remembering where I stopped helps; putting that spot in front of me would help more.

**Would he return?** Yes, with a guitar and metronome, for one concrete check: does E land on the third click while G rings through the second? No guitar practice, motor learning or delayed retention was measured.

## Priya — first-time setup and soil fungi

**What became easier.** The opening invitation to ask what she wanted to understand and the notebook-creation flow gave Priya a natural start without organizing a curriculum. Once a response arrived, it replaced her either/or question about fungi with understandable categories: decomposers, partners exchanging resources with roots, and contributors to soil structure.

> The explanation became useful when I could connect it to leaves, roots, and the soil I actually see.

She could repeat those categories, but had not demonstrated recognizing their roles in her own garden.

**What remained hard.** Priya could not tell which accounts, installation or subscription she needed, who would receive her question, what would be charged, or where the notebook lived. About you did not answer those questions. She would have stopped at the instruction to install Claude Code and run it in Terminal. The observer supplied an existing signed-in connection; she did not complete that setup herself.

Afterward, her visibly retained question was unavailable to Sophia. That made the meaning of a sent message unclear.

> If my question is still on the screen, I expect the app to help me continue from it.

**What would improve the next visit.** A first-run connection step before submission should explain the required account and installation, check the connection, and carry the question forward after setup. This supports the planned accountless product; it does not request a Sophia account system.

**Would she return?** Conditionally, once the connection is understandable and dependable. Her next question would be how to identify which fungal roles matter in her garden and which everyday gardening practice to investigate first. She still needs to turn a useful framework into careful observation.

## Implications for the next iteration

The strongest teaching moments used a small concrete example, an explicit contrast or a reason the learner could picture. They let the learner expose a specific uncertainty and respond with their own reasoning. The personas did not equate a fluent explanation with completed learning: derivation, source comparison, real instrument practice and garden observation all remained concrete unfinished work.

Their own words made saved pages useful to return to. The conversation supported inquiry; record management was useful when it preserved that inquiry accurately and distracting when it took over. The next-step needs differ: Elena wants a small derivation, Jamal needs inspected passages, Theo needs the practice location and a real attempt, and Priya needs a connection she can reach independently.

These retrospectives refine the existing tickets rather than adding duplicate issues. A human follow-up should test the specific next task for each persona and whether Sophia's support carries across a genuinely delayed return.
